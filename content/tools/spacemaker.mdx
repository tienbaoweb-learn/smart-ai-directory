## Overview

Spacemaker was the pioneering AI site planning platform that brought data-driven analysis to early-stage architectural and urban design — letting teams generate site layout options and evaluate them against sunlight, daylight, noise, wind, and density criteria. Acquired by Autodesk in 2020, Spacemaker's technology and approach now continue as **Autodesk Forma**, where its capabilities have been expanded within the Autodesk ecosystem.

The Spacemaker approach changed how early-stage site decisions get made: instead of committing to a massing concept based on intuition and testing it late, teams evaluate many options against measurable environmental and density criteria upfront — when changes are cheap and the impact on project outcomes is largest.

This review covers the Spacemaker approach and its current form in Autodesk Forma, for teams evaluating AI-driven early-stage design in 2026.

---

## Key Features

### AI Site Analysis

Spacemaker's core innovation — now in Forma — is rapid environmental analysis of site conditions: sun hours, daylight potential, noise exposure, wind comfort, and views, computed in minutes rather than the days that specialist consultants traditionally required.

### Generative Site Layouts

The platform generates site layout and massing options within defined constraints — units, height limits, density targets — giving teams a broad option space to evaluate rather than the handful of schemes manual design produces.

### Criteria-Based Option Comparison

Options are compared side-by-side against measurable criteria, turning early design discussions from subjective preference into data-supported decisions about which directions best balance density, livability, and site conditions.

### Continuation in Autodesk Forma

As Autodesk Forma, the platform connects early-stage analysis to the broader Autodesk ecosystem — including pathways toward Revit — extending Spacemaker's concept-stage strength into the downstream design workflow.

## Who Is It Best For?

The Spacemaker approach (via Autodesk Forma) is right if you:

- **Make early-stage site decisions** where environmental analysis should inform massing direction
- **Develop residential or mixed-use projects** where density and livability trade-offs matter
- **Want data-supported client and stakeholder conversations** at concept stage
- **Work in the Autodesk ecosystem** and want early-stage tools that connect downstream

It is **not** the right fit if you:

- Need detailed design and construction documentation (that's Revit's role, not Forma's)
- Work on small single-building projects where site option analysis adds little
- Expected a standalone Spacemaker product — it now exists only as Autodesk Forma
