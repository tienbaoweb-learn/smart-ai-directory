## Overview

goHeather is an AI-powered contract review tool built by a team of lawyers with over two decades of combined experience, designed to analyze contracts in minutes — detecting red flags, extracting key clauses, and summarizing complex documents. Rather than a general-purpose AI applying generic reasoning to a legal document, goHeather is specifically trained on legal data across contract types like purchase and sale agreements, rental agreements, NDAs, and contractor agreements.

For real estate professionals, contractors, and architects who regularly deal with contracts as part of closing deals, hiring vendors, or signing with clients, this gives a fast, affordable first pass before — not instead of — involving an attorney on anything significant.

This review evaluates goHeather based on its contract analysis accuracy, document workflow, and practical fit as a first-line legal review tool heading into 2026.

---

## Key Features

### Automated Risk Scoring

goHeather analyzes contracts and assigns automated risk scores, flagging concerning clauses rather than just producing a flat summary that leaves the user to spot issues themselves.

### Lawyer-Trained AI Models

The platform's AI is purpose-built and trained specifically on legal data by a team with real legal practice experience, rather than being a general AI model applied to legal documents without specialized training.

### Word & PDF Review Workflows

Contracts can be reviewed directly in Word with auto-generated redlines and suggested new clauses, or uploaded as a PDF through the web app, fitting into however a user already handles contract documents.

### Contract Generation

Beyond reviewing existing agreements, goHeather can generate a complete contract draft from scratch based on uploaded deal documents or a chat-based conversation about the deal terms.

## Pros & Cons

## Pros

- ✅ Trained by lawyers with over two decades of combined legal experience
- ✅ Detects red flags with automated risk scoring rather than just summarizing text
- ✅ Reviews directly in Word with auto-redlines and suggested clauses, or via PDF in the web app
- ✅ Covers a wide range of contract types relevant to real estate and contracting work

## Cons

- ❌ Explicitly not a substitute for a lawyer — output is legal information, not legal advice
- ❌ Best used as a first-pass filter before, not instead of, attorney review on major deals
- ❌ Accuracy depends on document clarity and quality of the uploaded contract
- ❌ Complex, highly negotiated agreements still benefit from experienced human judgment

---

## Who Is It Best For?

goHeather is the right tool if you:

- **Review contracts regularly** — purchase agreements, rentals, vendor deals — and want a fast first pass
- **Want red flags surfaced automatically** rather than reading every clause manually
- **Need a starting contract draft** rather than writing one from a blank page
- **Understand it's a starting point**, with a lawyer still involved for significant agreements

goHeather is **not** the right tool if you:

- Are looking for a full substitute for legal counsel on high-stakes contracts
- Have highly unusual or heavily negotiated terms that need experienced legal judgment
- Want guaranteed legal advice rather than AI-generated legal information

---

## Alternatives to Consider

goHeather is strong for fast, lawyer-trained contract review, but it is not the only option:

- **Signeasy** — better suited for the signing and document workflow once a contract is finalized
- **Setmore** — relevant for scheduling meetings around a deal rather than reviewing its terms
- **Acadium** — useful for marketing support rather than legal document review
- **Homesage AI** — relevant for property investment analysis rather than contract terms
- **DiedInHouse.com** — useful for property history research rather than contract review

---

## Final Verdict

goHeather earns its place as a fast, affordable first line of contract review for real estate professionals, contractors, and architects who deal with agreements regularly. The lawyer-trained risk scoring and clause extraction surface issues that might otherwise require a careful manual read-through, saving real time before a contract reaches an attorney.

The tool is explicit about its own limits — it provides legal information, not legal advice — and that distinction matters most on contracts with real financial or legal stakes.

**Our recommendation:** Use goHeather as your first-pass review on routine contracts, and route anything it flags as high-risk, or any significant deal, to an actual attorney before signing.
