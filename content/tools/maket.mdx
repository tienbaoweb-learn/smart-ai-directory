## Overview

Maket is an AI-powered generative design tool focused on the front of the architectural process: turning written requirements into 2D floor plan options. Instead of starting from a blank canvas in CAD, a user describes what the space needs — room counts, approximate dimensions, and adjacencies — and Maket generates layout variations to react to and refine.

The tool targets the earliest, most exploratory phase of a project, where the value is in quickly comparing spatial options rather than producing polished documentation. For architects, designers, and developers, that early layout exploration is often slow and repetitive when done by hand; Maket aims to compress it.

This review evaluates Maket based on its floor plan generation, iteration workflow, and practical fit for concept-stage design in 2026.

---

## Key Features

### Requirement-Driven Floor Plan Generation

Maket's core capability is generating 2D floor plans from plain-language requirements — room counts, sizes, and how spaces should relate to each other — producing several layout options rather than a single answer.

### Fast Layout Iteration

Because plans are generated rather than drawn, testing a different program or adjacency is quick. This suits the feasibility and concept stages, where comparing options matters more than detail.

### Design and Material Suggestions

Beyond layouts, Maket offers design and material suggestions that help move from an abstract brief toward a more concrete direction before detailed design begins.

### Browser-Based and Accessible

Maket runs in the browser with no install, making it approachable for non-CAD users and useful for early conversations with clients or stakeholders who can't read technical drawings.

## Pros & Cons

## Pros

- ✅ Generates multiple 2D floor plan options from written requirements like room counts and adjacencies
- ✅ Fast iteration at the earliest concept stage, when exploring layout options has the most leverage
- ✅ Browser-based and approachable for non-CAD users, with no software install
- ✅ Design and material suggestions help move from a blank brief toward a concrete direction

## Cons

- ❌ Generated plans are early-stage concepts, not construction-ready or code-checked documents
- ❌ Spatial and structural feasibility still needs an architect's review before detailed design
- ❌ Focused on layout generation rather than rendering or visualization
- ❌ Output quality depends on how clearly the requirements and constraints are specified

---

## Who Is It Best For?

Maket is the right tool if you:

- **Explore floor plan options early** and want to compare layouts before committing to CAD or BIM
- **Work at the feasibility stage** on unit mix, room programs, or adjacency studies
- **Want a fast spatial starting point** from a written brief rather than a blank canvas
- **Involve non-technical stakeholders** who respond better to generated layouts than to technical drawings

Maket is **not** the right tool if you:

- Need construction-ready, code-compliant documentation rather than concept layouts
- Are looking primarily for photorealistic rendering rather than layout generation
- Require precise structural or MEP coordination at this stage

## Alternatives to Consider

Maket is strong for early floor plan generation, but it is not the only option:

- **[ArchitectGPT](/tools/architectgpt)** — focuses on rendering and visualizing a design from an image rather than generating the layout itself
- **TestFit** — better for developer-driven feasibility with unit counts, parking, and cost metrics
- **Autodesk Forma** — site and massing analysis within the Autodesk ecosystem
- **ArkDesign AI** — generative design aimed at residential and multifamily layouts
- **[Planner 5D](/tools/planner-5d)** — layout and interior design tool with 2D/3D editing

---

## Final Verdict

Maket earns its place for architects, designers, and developers who want to generate and compare floor plan layouts at the concept stage, rather than drawing every option by hand. Its value is highest at the front of a project, where exploring spatial directions quickly has the most leverage on the eventual design.

As with other AI design tools, the output is a starting point, not a deliverable — generated plans need an architect's judgment on feasibility and code before detailed design proceeds.

**Our recommendation:** Use Maket to generate several layout directions from your brief, then take the strongest option into your CAD or BIM workflow.
