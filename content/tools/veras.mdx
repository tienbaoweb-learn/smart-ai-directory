## Overview

Veras, by EvolveLAB, is an AI-powered visualization tool that plugs directly into Revit, SketchUp, and Rhino, as well as Forma and the web. Rather than requiring an export to a separate rendering application, Veras captures the current viewport of your model and uses AI, guided by text prompts, to transform it into a styled render while preserving the actual geometry you've already modeled.

This integration is the core of its appeal: architects and designers can generate a polished, photorealistic visual directly from the software they're already working in, without the workflow interruption of exporting geometry to a standalone renderer.

This review evaluates Veras based on its rendering quality, geometry fidelity, and practical fit inside existing BIM and modeling workflows heading into 2026.

---

## Key Features

### In-App Viewport Rendering

Veras runs as a plugin inside Revit, SketchUp, and Rhino, capturing the current viewport and rendering it with AI directly inside the application you're already using — no export or round-trip required.

### Geometry Override Slider

This feature lets users adjust how closely the AI follows the underlying model geometry, balancing creative exploration against precision when the goal is a more literal representation of the actual design.

### Render Selection

Rather than regenerating an entire image to change one detail, Render Selection lets users select a portion of the render and redefine it with a new prompt, refining specific areas without starting over.

### Multi-Platform Support

Veras supports a wide range of host applications and recent software versions, making it practical to adopt across a design team using different primary modeling tools.

## Pros & Cons

## Pros

- ✅ Plugs directly into Revit, SketchUp, and Rhino — no exporting required
- ✅ Preserves the geometry of your actual model rather than reinterpreting it loosely
- ✅ Render Selection lets you re-prompt and regenerate just part of an image
- ✅ Geometry Override Slider gives control over how literally the model is followed

## Cons

- ❌ Subscription pricing per user can add up for larger teams
- ❌ Output quality still depends on the quality of the underlying model and viewport
- ❌ Best results require some iteration on prompts and settings
- ❌ Limited to supported host applications rather than working as a standalone tool

---

## Who Is It Best For?

Veras is the right tool if you:

- **Already model in Revit, SketchUp, or Rhino** and want rendering without leaving the app
- **Need renders that stay faithful to your actual model geometry**, not a loose interpretation
- **Want to iterate quickly** on specific parts of a render rather than regenerating everything
- **Work across a design team using multiple modeling tools**

Veras is **not** the right tool if you:

- Don't use one of its supported host applications
- Need a fully standalone rendering tool independent of a modeling platform
- Have a tight per-seat budget and a large team that would need multiple licenses

---

## Alternatives to Consider

Veras is strong for in-app, geometry-faithful AI rendering, but it is not the only option:

- **D5 Render** — full real-time rendering engine with deeper lighting and material control
- **ArchiVinci** — browser-based, no plugin or modeling software required
- **Vizcom** — broader sketch-to-render tool popular beyond architecture alone
- **Midjourney** — more flexible and creative, with less structural fidelity to the original model
- **Archicad AI** — full BIM authoring platform with AI-assisted modeling, not just rendering

---

## Final Verdict

Veras earns its place for architects and designers who want AI rendering without breaking their existing modeling workflow. Working directly inside Revit, SketchUp, or Rhino — and actually respecting the geometry already modeled — sets it apart from more general image generators like [Midjourney](/tools/midjourney) that treat architectural input as loose inspiration.

The per-seat subscription cost is worth weighing against team size, and results still benefit from some prompt iteration to dial in the desired look.

**Our recommendation:** Trial Veras directly inside your primary modeling tool on a real project view to evaluate render quality and geometry fidelity before rolling it out across your full design team.
