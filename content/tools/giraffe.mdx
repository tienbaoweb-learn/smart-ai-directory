## Overview

Giraffe is a browser-based platform that combines mapping, sketching, and live analytics into one workspace, letting architects, urban planners, and developers explore and communicate design ideas directly on real-world maps. Built for city planning, it sits at the site and portfolio scale rather than the individual building.

Its strength is bringing GIS data and feasibility analytics together with quick massing and layout sketching, so teams can assess zoning, flood, contours, and financial viability in the same place they draw. It has been used for feasibility studies, land-use planning, master planning, and development tracking.

This review evaluates Giraffe on its planning, GIS, and feasibility capabilities and its fit for early-stage development work in 2026.

---

## Key Features

### Map-Based Sketching

Users draw massing, road networks, parking, and layouts directly within a GIS context, combining design and site data in one view.

### GIS Data Layering

Giraffe layers zoning, flood, contours, parcels, solar, and other site data so plans are assessed against real constraints from the start.

### Feasibility and Financials

A suite of feasibility tools covers take-offs and financial viability, plus portfolio tools to understand an investment thesis across sites.

### Cloud Collaboration and Sharing

As a browser-based platform, it supports shared projects for client, consultant, and public engagement without desktop GIS setup.

- ✅ Strong GIS layering: zoning, flood, contours, parcels, solar, and site data

## Cons

- ❌ Full value assumes comfort working with GIS data and site context
- ❌ Per-seat annual pricing suits firms more than occasional individual users
- ❌ Not a rendering or BIM tool — it complements rather than replaces them

---

## Who Is It Best For?

Giraffe is the right tool if you:

- **Do master planning or urban planning** in a GIS context
- **Run early feasibility** and site analysis on development sites
- **Explore massing and zoning** at the site or portfolio scale
- **Need shared, map-based analytics** for engagement

Giraffe is **not** the right tool if you:

- Need detailed building design, BIM, or construction documentation
- Want photorealistic rendering rather than planning analytics
- Are an occasional individual user for whom per-seat pricing is hard to justify

## Final Verdict

Giraffe earns its place for planners, developers, and architects working at the site and portfolio scale who want mapping, sketching, and feasibility analytics in one browser-based tool. Bringing GIS context and financial viability together with quick massing is genuinely useful in the earliest phase of a project.

It isn't a building-design or rendering tool, so it works best alongside BIM and visualization software rather than replacing them.
